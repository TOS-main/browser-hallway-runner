For Parents & Teachers

How Hallway Dash is set up to be safe for students of every age, in plain language.

Hallway Dash is a cartoon running game set in a school, and a lot of the people who play it are students. We built it with that in mind from day one. This page explains, without legal language, what the game does for each age group, what it keeps, where ads appear and how the online parts are kept safe. The full details are in our privacy policy.

What kind of game is it?

Hallway Dash is a three-lane endless runner. Players run down a school hallway avoiding students, teachers and objects, collect coins and spend them on characters and cosmetics. There is no violence beyond cartoon bumps and stumbles: if you run into someone, they chase you for a few seconds, and if they catch you the run ends. There's no chat, no voice, no user-uploaded images and no way to message other players.

The game includes an optional math "pop quiz" that appears during some runs, which can be switched off in Settings.

The age check

The first time the game opens, it asks the player's age with a neutral slider. We keep only the age group (under 13, 13 to 15, or 16 and over), never the exact age. That group decides how the game is set up:

For every age, we add each visit to an anonymous daily total (how many visits and minutes of play, by age group) so we know how many people play. It contains no player id, name or device details, so no one can be told apart.

Where ads appear

Hallway Dash is free, and ads help pay for it. For players 13 and up, ads appear in only two places:

  • When the player chooses to watch one, for example to revive or to get a free box. Nothing happens unless they tap the button.
  • In a short break between some runs: never in the first few runs or the tutorial. Any purchase in the School Store turns these off permanently.

There are no pop-ups over gameplay and no banner ads on this website's guide pages.

Usernames and school leaderboards

Players who play online pick a username, which is how others see them on leaderboards. The game asks them not to use their real name, runs usernames through a word filter, and only allows a change once a month. Players can join their school's board so their runs count toward the school's score. The rules are shown before joining:

  • Use a username, never your real name.
  • Don't share an address, phone number, social media or anyone else's information.
  • School names must be the school's real name, and school boards never show a town, address or class.
  • No bad words, bullying or hate.

Every name on a board has a 🚩 Report button and a block option. Reports go only to the game's moderator, who also checks every new school name before anyone else can see it, and can hide players, schools or levels that break the rules.

Saving and deleting data

Progress saves automatically in the browser. Nothing else is needed. Players 13 and up can optionally sign in with Google to carry progress to another device, or copy a save code as a backup.

To delete everything, open Settings → Privacy → Delete all my data. This wipes the progress on that device, the online leaderboard entry and any cloud save. You can also email us (address below) with the subject "Delete my Hallway Dash data" and we'll delete it within 30 days. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information, email us and we'll remove it.

Using Hallway Dash at school

The game runs in any modern browser, including school Chromebooks, with no install and no account. It's a good fit for a short brain break. Some ideas teachers have suggested:

  • Use the Duel a Friend code so the whole class runs the exact same hallway and compares scores.
  • Leave pop quizzes on for quick mental-math practice during runs.
  • Try the Level Maker as a design exercise: students plan a hallway that's challenging but fair, then have to beat it themselves before sharing.

If your school blocks the site and you'd like it unblocked, the domain to allow is hallwaydash.org.
