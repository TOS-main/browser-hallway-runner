How to Play Hallway Dash

Everything a new runner needs: the controls, the three kinds of obstacles, chases, combos and how to keep a run going.

The goal

You are a student who is about to be late. In Infinite Run, the classic mode, the goal is simply to get as far down the hallway as you can. The hallway never ends, it slowly speeds up, and your score climbs with every metre you run, every coin you grab and every trick you pull off. In the Campaign, each stage has a finish line (your classroom) and a bell timer instead: reach the door before the bell rings.

The hallway has three lanes. Your runner moves forward on their own; you only decide which lane to be in and when to jump or slide. That's what makes Hallway Dash easy to pick up and hard to master: there are only four moves, but the hallway throws them at you faster and faster.

Controls

Phone, tablet or touch screen

  • Swipe left or right to switch lanes. You can start a swipe anywhere on the screen, even on top of a button.
  • Swipe up to jump. If you swipe up a moment before you land, you jump again as soon as your feet touch the floor.
  • Swipe down to slide. Swiping down in mid-air drops you to the floor quickly and goes straight into a slide.
  • ✋ button to push people out of your lane, 👟 button (or a double-tap) to put on Heel Wheels, and 💥 button for the combo finisher when your combo reaches x3.

Left-handed? Settings has a left-handed button layout, and you can drag the run buttons to wherever your thumbs are comfortable.

You can also play with a mouse: click and drag in a direction works exactly like a swipe.

The three kinds of obstacles

Every obstacle in Hallway Dash belongs to one of three families, and each family has one answer. Learning to sort obstacles instantly is the single biggest step from beginner to good player.

  1. Low obstacles: jump. Things on the floor, like trash cans, wet-floor signs, piles of dropped backpacks, mop buckets, spilled lunch trays and the robotics club robot. Hitting one trips you.
  2. High obstacles: slide. Things at head height, like hanging spirit banners, giant posters students are carrying, a painter's ladder or a locker door popping open. Hitting one bonks you.
  3. Blockers: switch lanes. People and big objects that fill the lane, like a group of chatting students, a class walking in line, a teacher, a hall monitor or a teacher pushing the AV cart. Running into a blocker head-on ends your run.

The first time you meet a new obstacle, the game shows its name and what to do about it. Our obstacle guide lists every one.

Bumps and chases

Not every hit ends your run. If you clip something from the side, for example by switching lanes into a student who is right next to you, you stumble and whatever you bumped starts chasing you for 10 seconds. A warning shows who is after you. During that time, any other hit means you're caught. Stay clean for ten seconds and they give up.

Swiping toward the wall when you're already in the outside lane slams the lockers, and the locker's owner comes after you too. Some campaign stages and boss stages start a chase that never stops, so a single mistake is the end.

Special moves

Push

Tap the ✋ button (or Q) to shove the people in front of you out of your lane. Pushing recharges after a few seconds, and the Push Power upgrade makes it recharge faster. It's your emergency exit when all three lanes look blocked.

Heel Wheels

Double-tap the screen (or press E) to put on Heel Wheels. You skate faster, can do tricks when you jump, and the wheels save you from one crash. You start with a free pair, and you can buy or earn more.

Head hops

Time a jump so you land on someone's head and you bounce off for bonus points: a Head Hop. Land on another head before you touch the floor and the chain grows: five in a row earns the "Crowd Surfer" title. Look out for lines of students waiting for the water fountain; they're spaced perfectly for hopping. Only the three-high cheer pyramid can't be hopped.

Combos and the finisher

Close calls, dodges and tricks build your combo. Every few in a row raises the combo multiplier, up to x3. At x3 the 💥 finisher lights up: press it (or X) and every obstacle on screen goes flying for a big score bonus. If you go too long without a trick, the combo resets.

Coins, stars and score

  • Coins (and the occasional dollar bill) are your cash for the Locker Room: runners, outfits, upgrades and boosts.
  • Gold Stars are rare. They show up during runs and from rewards, and buy revives and premium runners.
  • Outfit Tokens buy outfits for your runner, like Varsity, Neon Night or Golden.
  • Letter tiles spell the Daily Word. Collect all the letters in one day for a bonus.
  • Your score grows with distance and is multiplied by your score multiplier, which goes up each time you finish a set of three Missions (up to x30). Harder difficulties and faster speeds multiply it further.

How a run ends

A run ends when you run head-on into a blocker, when you get caught during a chase, or, in campaign stages, when the bell rings before you reach class. When that happens you can spend a Gold Star to revive and carry on from where you were. Watching a short optional ad is another way to revive (for players 13 and older on the website).

Your first ten runs

  1. Play the tutorial all the way through. It takes about a minute and teaches each move with a real obstacle.
  2. Stay in the middle lane when you can. It gives you two escape routes instead of one.
  3. Follow the coins. Coin lines are always placed where a runner can actually go.
  4. Ignore the score for now and just watch what's coming. Your eyes should be halfway down the hallway, not on your runner.
  5. After a few runs, open Missions and try to finish all three. Raising your multiplier early pays off for every run after.
  6. Spend your first coins on the Hall Pass upgrade, then try the Campaign for steady rewards.
