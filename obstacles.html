Obstacle Guide

Every obstacle in Hallway Dash, sorted by what you do about it: jump, slide or switch lanes.

Hallway Dash has more than forty obstacles, and they change depending on where you are: elementary hallways have little kids and science-fair volcanoes, high school has teachers and AV carts, the college campus has sleep-deprived students and runaway e-scooters. The good news is that every single one falls into one of three groups, and each group has exactly one answer.

The golden rule: low things get jumped (swipe up), high things get slid under (swipe down), and people or big carts get avoided (switch lanes or push). Running into a blocker head-on ends your run.

Low obstacles: jump over them

Low obstacles sit on the floor. Swipe up (or press ↑) a little before you reach them. With Super Sneakers or the Spring Insoles upgrade your jumps go higher, which gives you more room for error.

High obstacles: slide under them

High obstacles hang or stick out at head height. Swipe down (or press ↓) to slide. If you're in the air, swiping down slams you to the floor and goes straight into a slide, which is the fix when you jumped too early.

Giant spirit posterEverywhereCarried by students, so it moves toward you. Slide a little earlier than you would for a banner.

Blockers: switch lanes

Blockers fill their whole lane and can't be jumped or slid past, except by head-hopping when you're high enough. Hit them head-on and your run is over. Clip them from the side and they chase you for 10 seconds.

BlockerTip
Group of chatting studentsThe most common blocker. They stand still, so commit to your lane change early.
Two students walking and talkingWalk toward you slowly. They close the gap faster than a standing group.
A class walking in lineLong. Don't switch back into their lane until the last kid has passed.
Student digging in their lockerOnly in the outside lanes. Another reason to prefer the middle.
Student texting while walkingNot looking where they're going. Treat them like any moving blocker.
Student sprinting the other wayFast! React as soon as you see them. A good push target.
Little kidElementary only. Small, but still a blocker. You can head hop them.
Marching band drummerOften several in a row. Find the open lane and stay in it.
Cheer squad pyramidThree people high: the one blocker you can't head hop. Always go around.
Teacher pushing the AV cartWide and slow. Switch lanes early.

Students with open umbrellasRainy Day stages. Wide and hard to see past.
Sleep-deprived studentCollege Campus. Wanders slowly; easy to plan around.

Teachers, monitors and the people who chase you

Some blockers are adults with opinions about running in the halls:

  • Strict teacher (middle and high school) may step into your lane yelling "NO RUNNING!". Watch their feet: when they start to move, pick a different lane.
  • Ms. Moore, the little teacher, is small enough to surprise you. "STOP! It's the little teacher!"
  • Hall monitor appears in every school and is the classic chaser. In boss stages a monitor rides a scooter.
  • Strict professor replaces teachers on the College Campus.
  • The coach patrols After-School Athletics.

Whoever you bump becomes your chaser for 10 seconds. In longer Infinite Runs, a hunter (the Principal, a hall monitor or the bully) also sets off after you from time to time; you'll see a warning and a red lane to stay out of.

Things you can run on

  • Rows of lunch tables: jump up onto the first one and run along the tops. There are usually coins up there and nothing to trip on.
  • Bleachers and risers: step up and run across the top of the crowd.
  • People's heads: land on a head to bounce (a Head Hop) and chain them for big points.

Special hallway pieces

  • Classroom shortcut: a yellow "SHORTCUT +COINS" door. Run through it for a stretch of coins with no obstacles. You can't switch lanes while you're inside.
  • Freshly waxed floor: a shiny, slippery patch that changes how your lane switches feel. Don't panic-swipe on it.
  • Pop quiz: a quick math question pops up and three answer boards appear, one per lane. Run through the right answer for bonus points, coins and a combo boost. Quizzes appear in Infinite Run, and you can turn them off in Settings.
  • Finish gate: the classroom door at the end of a campaign stage.

Event and weather obstacles

Limited-time events bring their own obstacle: snowmen during Snow Day, fire hoses during Fire Drill Week, hurdles during Hall Pass Rush, spilled trays during the Cafeteria Food Fight, balloon arches on Prom Night and jack-o'-lanterns during the Halloween Hallway. Rainy Day stages on the College Campus add puddles and umbrellas, and Night School stages dim the lights.

Which obstacles appear where

Each school has its own mix. Elementary School is the gentlest: lots of little kids, classes in line, backpacks and volcanoes, with wide lanes. Middle School adds walking pairs, rushers, teachers, the robot and painter's ladders. High School is busier and faster, with more teachers, AV carts, cheer pyramids and band drummers. The College Campus is outdoors, with professors, sleepy students and scooters crossing your path. After-School Athletics swaps the hallway for the gym: basketballs, sprinklers, hurdles and the coach. See the campaign guide for more on each school.
